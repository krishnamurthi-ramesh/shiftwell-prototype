\section*{Deliverable 1: Recommendation Memo to the CEO (Page 1 of 2)}

\begin{infocard}
\footnotesize

\textbf{DATE:} October 18, 2026 \quad\textbar\quad \textbf{SUBJECT:} Q4 2026 Engineering Build Recommendation: What to Ship, What to Defer, and Why
\end{infocard}

\subsection*{1. Executive Summary \& The Core Diagnosis}
Our three largest accounts -- FreshBowl (\INR 84L ARR), CarePoint (\INR 60L ARR), and UrbanMart (\INR 110L ARR) -- have submitted urgent, divergent feature requests. Sales recommends building all three. However, engineering capacity is strictly capped at \textbf{6 sprints} (2 engineers, 1 designer). The three requested custom builds total \textbf{6 sprints} (WhatsApp: 3, Overtime: 2, Certificates: 1), consuming 100\% of capacity with zero contingency for QA, edge cases, or platform scale.

A rigorous diagnostic of customer interview transcripts and churn data uncovers the fundamental truth:
\begin{calloutcard}{BrandRose}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Root-Cause Finding:} Customers ask for communication channels (WhatsApp) and isolated rules, but their existential pain is \textbf{unbearable manager approval latency}. Every swap currently requires manual sign-off by a Location Manager.
\end{calloutcard}

Across our active and churned accounts, \textbf{Swap Approval Speed (<24 hours) is the single leading indicator of retention and churn}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Healthy Accounts} (UrbanMart, QuickKart, MedAxis): Average \textbf{70\%} of swaps resolved in <24 hours.
    \item \textbf{At-Risk Accounts} (FreshBowl, FitZone, Brew \& Co): Average only \textbf{34\%} of swaps resolved in <24 hours.
    \item \textbf{Churned Accounts} (TandooriTown, GreenLeaf, PawsCare): Averaged \textbf{28.7\%} prior to churn.
\end{itemize}

Every single account lost in the past 12 months had approval speeds under 35\%. GreenLeaf's exit interview proves the point: \textit{``Honestly it wasn't WhatsApp. Our managers couldn't keep up with approvals, and RotaPro let swaps go through automatically if the person was trained.''} WhatsApp merely moves an unmanageable queue from mobile app to chat; it does nothing to clear it.

\subsection*{2. Strategic Recommendation: Build the Rules-Based Auto-Approval Engine}
Instead of three bespoke, disconnected features, I recommend engineering a single, configurable \textbf{Rules-Based Auto-Approval Engine} this quarter (\textbf{4 sprints total}). The engine evaluates incoming bilateral swap requests in real time against policy rules. When all active conditions pass, swaps resolve instantly without human friction.

\section*{Deliverable 1: Recommendation Memo to the CEO (Page 2 of 2)}

\subsection*{3. Strategic \& Economic Justification}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\Alph*.}]
    \item \textbf{Eliminates Root-Cause Churn across \INR 1.84 Cr of At-Risk ARR:}
    FreshBowl (\INR 84L, renewal Jan 2027), CarePoint (\INR 60L, Dec 2026), FitZone (\INR 22L, Oct 2026), and Brew \& Co (\INR 18L, Nov 2026) all suffer from manager scheduling bottlenecks (averaging 5.5--7.0 hrs/week). By auto-resolving ~75\% of swaps instantly, manager administrative load drops by ~65\%, eliminating shift dropouts and protecting \INR 1.84 Cr in imminent renewals.
    \item \textbf{Neutralizes Competitor Threat (RotaPro):}
    Sales fears RotaPro's WhatsApp feature. However, RotaPro won GreenLeaf because of \textit{automatic approvals for qualified staff}. By shipping our Rules Engine, we neutralize RotaPro's operational advantage without incurring WhatsApp API messaging costs (\INR 0.35/message across 9,160 monthly swaps = \INR 38,000+/year saved).
    \item \textbf{A Scalable Platform vs. Fragile Tech Debt:}
    Building custom one-offs for UrbanMart and CarePoint creates maintenance liabilities. A rules engine is a reusable architecture: any enterprise customer can configure customized rules (e.g., rest periods, union seniority, minor labor laws) in future quarters.
    \item \textbf{Built-in 2-Sprint Safety Buffer:}
    Building 3 disparate features consumes 6/6 sprints (100\% capacity). With the engineering lead on leave until next week, zero buffer guarantees slips and poor QA. Our plan consumes 4 sprints, preserving 2 full sprints for integration testing, edge cases, and pilot rollouts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{4. What We Will NOT Build This Quarter (and Why)}
\begin{calloutcard}{BrandAmber}
\footnotesize
\textbf{Deferred Initiative:} WhatsApp Swap Request \& Approval Integration (3 Sprints).
\end{calloutcard}
\textbf{Rationale:} WhatsApp is a delivery channel, not a decision engine. At FreshBowl, store managers receive 25--30 swap requests every night. Sending 30 WhatsApp notifications per night to an overworked manager still results in delays, ignored messages, and burnout. Once our Rules Engine auto-approves 75\% of swaps, the manager only receives 3--5 genuine exception requests per week. We will re-evaluate WhatsApp in Q1 2027 specifically for \textit{manager escalation alerts}, where it delivers genuine high-leverage utility.

\subsection*{5. Account-by-Account Stakeholder Communication Plan}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FreshBowl (\INR 84L ARR \textbar\ Restaurants \textbar\ Jan 2027 Renewal):}
    \textit{``We investigated your frontline feedback. WhatsApp still forces your store managers to process 30 approvals every night. Instead, we are shipping instant Auto-Approval for trained station cooks. Your managers won't have to touch 80\% of requests. Swaps resolve in seconds, cooks don't drop shifts, and your managers get back 5 hours every week.''}
    \item \textbf{CarePoint Clinics (\INR 60L ARR \textbar\ Healthcare \textbar\ Dec 2026 Renewal):}
    \textit{``Your compliance vulnerability is solved. We are shipping an integrated Certificate Expiry Enforcement Module this quarter. Any nurse with an expired BLS or Council registration is automatically blocked from scheduling or swapping. Your clinic managers no longer need manual Excel sheets, and audit penalties are 100\% eliminated.''}
    \item \textbf{UrbanMart (\INR 110L ARR \textbar\ Retail \textbar\ Aug 2027 Renewal):}
    \textit{``Your overtime cost control is delivered. Our new Rules Engine allows you to enforce your 48-hour weekly cap. Any swap breaching 48 hours is instantly flagged and escalated to Regional Managers for 1-click authorization with overtime cost projections.''}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{6. Decision Summary}
Build the \textbf{Rules-Based Auto-Approval Engine}. It protects \INR 1.84 Cr in renewal ARR, eliminates the root cause of customer churn, solves UrbanMart and CarePoint's explicit requirements, beats RotaPro at their own game, and leaves a 2-sprint buffer to guarantee flawless execution.

\section*{Deliverable 3: Assumptions List (Where Data Was Missing)}

In compliance with the case brief, all assumptions formulated where data was unstated are categorized below:

\subsection*{A. Engineering Sizing \& Technical Architecture Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sprint Velocity \& Capacity:} Engineering capacity is fixed at 6 sprints for Q4 (2 engineers, 1 designer). I assumed full developer availability without unexpected sick leave or unplanned infrastructure incidents.
    \item \textbf{Core Rules Engine Scope (~3 sprints):} Engineering notes quote 2 sprints for UrbanMart's specific overtime approval and 1 sprint for CarePoint's certificate field. I assumed standardizing these into a generalized, rule-evaluating engine takes 3 sprints, since the core pipeline logic (rule evaluation hook, status updates, notification dispatch) is built once and shared.
    \item \textbf{Absence of Existing Rule Infrastructure:} As confirmed by engineering (``every swap goes to Location Manager... there are no rules; this is hard-coded''), I assumed zero legacy rule infrastructure exists. The engine must intercept swap requests before they hit the database approval queue.
    \item \textbf{Mobile App Decoupling:} I assumed the mobile app only needs to display a new \texttt{Auto-Approved} status badge for swaps; no complex client-side architecture rewrites are required for frontline staff.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{B. Customer Behavior \& Churn Dynamics Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Auto-Approval Rate (~75\%):} FreshBowl's store manager states: \textit{``I approve almost all of them without thinking. I only check if they're trained for the station and won't go into overtime.''} Based on this, I assumed that 75\%--80\% of routine shift swaps satisfy role and overtime rules and can safely bypass manual review.
    \item \textbf{CarePoint Renewal Risk is Regulatory, Not Dissatisfaction:} CarePoint boasts a healthy 71\% approval speed and low scheduling hours (4.0 hrs/week). Their ``At Risk'' status is entirely triggered by audit exposure (two shifts covered by nurses with expired BLS certificates). Solving credential blocking immediately secures their \INR 60L renewal.
    \item \textbf{UrbanMart Churn Imminence is Low:} UrbanMart's renewal is in August 2027 (10 months away), staff volume is healthy (5,200 staff), and swap approval speed is 64\%. The VP HR's statement that they would ``look at options'' is standard enterprise negotiation posturing rather than imminent churn. Supplying overtime rules in Q4 satisfies their demand with 10 months of retention buffer.
    \item \textbf{FitZone and Brew \& Co Root Cause:} Both accounts exhibit acute approval bottlenecks (35\% and 29\% <24h) and high manager workload (5.5 and 7.0 hrs/wk). I assumed their churn risk stems directly from the same manual approval gridlock affecting FreshBowl.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{C. Financial Modeling \& Unit Economics Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Manager Time Savings (~65\%):} Because ~75\% of monthly swap requests (portfolio total: 9,160 swaps/mo) will be resolved instantaneously without manager intervention, I assumed manager scheduling hours will decrease proportionally from an average of 4.75 hrs/week to ~1.7 hrs/week.
    \item \textbf{WhatsApp Cost Avoidance:} Engineering notes cite WhatsApp Business API messaging costs of \INR 0.35/message. Across 9,160 swaps/month (each requiring 2--3 template pings for request, approval, and confirmation), full adoption would incur \INR 75,000--\INR 1,15,000 annually in variable vendor costs. Auto-approval avoids this recurring cost.
\end{itemize}
